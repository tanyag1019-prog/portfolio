Executive summary

Around 17% of this e-commerce company's 5,630 customers were churning — well above typical benchmarks — with a retention budget too small to cover everyone. Scoring every customer on churn risk and value identified 157 "Honeymoon Exit" customers to prioritise: first-month, high-order customers who had already complained, churning at 66%. They held roughly ₹8 lakh in near-term order value — protectable through a ₹1.1 lakh campaign built on complaint resolution, an early-loyalty programme and mobile-first outreach. The model was validated on held-out data (AUC 0.847).

Counter-intuitive finding #1

Churn is an onboarding problem, not a loyalty one — each extra month of tenure cuts churn odds ~18%, and a missing tenure value alone roughly quadruples churn odds.

Counter-intuitive finding #2

Satisfaction score predicts churn in reverse — high scorers leave more, suggesting it's a one-time rating, not a loyalty measure.

Who got the budget — "The Honeymoon Exits"

  • First-month customers (median tenure: 1 month) who already complained — 87% of the segment vs 28.5% of the overall base.
  • Still highly active: median 7 orders in the last month, churning at 66% — 4x the 16.8% base rate.
  • ~76% buy electronics (higher-consideration purchases); ~69% log in on mobile; split across Tier 1 (53%) and Tier 3 (46%) cities.
  • Selected by scoring every customer on churn probability (>0.5) and order activity above median, then ranked by expected value at risk.

The marketing plan — Retain → Loyalty → Expand

01 · Retain

Tiered complaint resolution — personal calls for the top 50 value-at-risk complainers within 48hrs; a WhatsApp resolution flow for the rest. Gestures (service visit, warranty, delivery credit) over discounts.

02 · Loyalty

"Insider" membership pilot — wallet points, early access, contributor status — gamified around community, not discounts. Points expire in 60 days, framed as a gentle nudge.

03 · Expand

Personalised cross-sell on last purchase, delivered mobile-first (WhatsApp/push), capped at ~2 touches/week to protect a segment that has already complained once.

Budget & validation

Bottom-up campaign budget of ≈₹1.1 lakh (resolution calls, WhatsApp flows, service gestures, Insider pilot, cross-sell setup, contingency), breaking even at just 11 of 157 customers saved — well below the 78-customer target.

A held-out 20-customer control group tracks segment churn vs. control, complaint-resolution time, Insider enrolment and repeat-purchase rate — so impact is measurable, not assumed.

Exhibit — actual model output

Joint logistic regression model (statsmodels) — all six drivers plus missing-value flags, AUC 0.847 on a 30% held-out test set.

The takeover

Early 2024: a snaggle-toothed, bunny-eared monster hanging off Lisa's designer bag sparked a global frenzy. After she shared Labubu on Instagram, Pop Mart saw a 188% jump in net income. Rihanna, Dua Lipa and David Beckham fuelled the hype further — fans queued overnight, crashed restock pages, and resold rare Labubus for thousands, with one record auction sale hitting $150K.

From niche 2015 art-toy to 2025 global lifestyle icon — the product evolution behind the hype.

STP snapshot

Segmentation

Age 18–29, 75% female. Identity-seekers and collectors, FOMO-responsive, urban & globally mobile.

Targeting

Primary: Gen Z & Millennial "kidults" in Asia & the West. Secondary: fashion-forward influencers. Channel: social-first discovery → DTC.

Positioning

"The only designer toy that fuses art, scarcity and social currency into one ugly-cute cultural icon you wear as much as display."

Why it exploded

  • Engineered scarcity: blind boxes with "secret" variant odds as low as 1/144, timed drops and regional exclusives trigger queues, sell-outs and FOMO.
  • Social validation loop: celebrity co-signs → organic TikTok/Instagram virality → Reddit/Discord trading & authentication culture.
  • Resale as status signal: 10–20x typical markups, 1,000+ daily StockX sales, turning toys into tradeable assets.
  • Strategic collaborations: high-fashion houses, K-pop acts and luxury retailers (Harrods) turned Labubu into cultural currency.

Retail vs. resale price escalation across product tiers — the aftermarket that turned collecting into speculation.

Brand risk to watch

Single-IP dependency — Pop Mart's revenue leans heavily on Labubu, much like Molly before it. Trend fatigue and counterfeit proliferation are the next threats to manage.

Future strategy idea

An official secondary market (StockX/Christie's partnership) and a collector membership tier — converting trend buyers into long-term loyalists.

The mandate

Across 8 simulated rounds, Team Phoenix launched and managed smartphones across Europe & Asia, competing on pricing, portfolio, ESG and advertising against 5 rival teams — targeting 6 consumer segments from Late Adopters to Tech Savvy, with shareholder value as the ultimate KPI.

Share price trajectory, R1 → R6

R7 brought a margin trap (a €22/unit-margin SKU dragged blended GP% to 20.8%) before R8's disciplined recovery closed at €260.84 — still #1 on Round 8 share price and operating profit.

The pivot that won it

Round 4: while all 5 rivals fought over the Trendy + Enthusiast segments in Europe (~1,517k units combined), market research showed Price Conscious Asia + Socially Conscious demand of 20,000k+ units sitting almost untouched. Phoenix launched Zenith Air — a budget-friendly, first-mover product for that gap — tripled Asia channel investment, and rode the share price from €96.59 to €274.80, taking #1 in the Universe.

R8 recovery — 5 moves that fixed the margin trap

  • Dropped Zenith Neo (€22/unit margin) — freed up admin cost and pushed buyers to premium SKUs.
  • Raised repair margins 40%→65% (Europe) and 40%→55% (Asia) for +€21,281k repair GP.
  • Cut total marketing spend from €365k to €270k, matching the most efficient rival's ROI level.
  • Undercut competitor pricing on hero SKUs — EcoLite at €329 vs. €339, Aura at €699 vs. €899.

Reflection

Best call

Reading the market data to spot the Price Conscious + Socially Conscious gap in Round 4 — our single biggest source of alpha, and a first-mover advantage rivals only copied a round later.

Worst call

Launching Zenith Neo at a €22/unit margin in R7 — 1,006k units of volume that actively destroyed blended profitability. Should have modelled RoS impact before launch.

Key principle: profitability and share price move together — volume without margin is destruction, and timing of market entry matters as much as the entry itself.
